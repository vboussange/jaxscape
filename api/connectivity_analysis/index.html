    Connectivity analysis¤

    ConnectivityAnalysis computes the connectivity of a landscape raster: the sum of the proximities of its pairs of pixels, weighted by their habitat quality, where the proximity of two pixels is a decreasing function of their distance. SensitivityAnalysis computes the derivatives of this connectivity with respect to the quality and the permeability of each pixel. Both compute distances window by window: the memory of their distance computations is set by dependency_range and batch_size, independent of the raster size. The window operations serve to build other definitions of connectivity (see Window operations).

    Windowed analyses¤

    ConnectivityAnalysis and SensitivityAnalysis subclass WindowedAnalysis: they share its arguments, and its windows, coarsening, tiles, padding and permeability floor.

    jaxscape.windowed_analysis.WindowedAnalysis ¤

    Base class for windowed connectivity analyses on large rasters.

    Every pixel is the centre of a window of 2 * dependency_range + 1 pixels a side, and forms pairs with the pixels of its window only. The rasters are padded so that the windows of the pixels at the raster edges fit: the padding has quality 0 and the permeability floor. Permeability below the floor, 1e-4 times the maximum permeability, is raised to it: the graph of every window is connected, and regions of zero permeability stay nearly impassable.

    With coarsening, only the windows of block centres are computed, every b pixels along each axis and on the first and last pixels, where b is dependency_range * coarsening_factor rounded up to an odd integer. Values between block centres are interpolated bilinearly: the weight of a centre falls linearly from 1 at the centre to 0 at the neighbouring centres, along each axis, and the weights of all centres sum to 1 at every pixel.

    Block centres run in batches of at most batch_size. Distances whose queries from several sources share work, such as ResistanceDistance (see AbstractDistance.sources_share_work), compute a batch on tiles: a tile is the union of the windows of a grid of block centres, and one query on it gives each centre its distances to the pixels of its window. The graph of a tile extends beyond each window, so that the distances computed on it are lower than those computed on the window alone, and sensitivity maps have seams along the edges between tiles. Larger tiles cost less per block centre and leave fewer seams. Other distances, such as LCPDistance, compute the distances of each block centre on its own window. Randomized distances, such as ResistanceDistance with SpielmanApproximation, apply the proximity returned by AbstractDistance.debiased_proximity.

    An analysis builds its solver states once, and every run starts from them and reuses the compiled programs (see Reusing solver state).

    Arguments:

    • quality_raster: Habitat quality values. Both rasters take their common dtype, which is floating, and so do the results: an integer or boolean quality takes the floating dtype of the permeability, and a float32 raster with a float64 one gives float64.
    • permeability_raster: Movement permeability, or function quality -> permeability. An integer or boolean permeability takes JAX's default floating dtype.
    • distance: Distance, such as LCPDistance() or ResistanceDistance().
    • dependency_range: Window half-width D in pixels. Pairs farther apart than D along either axis do not contribute, and the default proximity decays over D / 4 equivalent pixels.
    • batch_size: Largest number of block centres per batch, which sets the memory: a batch holds its windows, or the distances that the queries on its tiles return. Exact ResistanceDistance and FreeEnergyDistance hold m * n distances for m sources and n tile vertices, besides the memory of the solver. With batch_size at least the number of block centres, one tile covers the raster.
    • proximity: Function mapping distances, in the units of distance, to proximities. Defaults to exp(-r / (D / 4)), where r is the distance in equivalent pixels: the number of pixels through habitat of permeability 1 that gives the same distance. See ConnectivityAnalysis for the units of each distance and the ecological rationale.
    • coarsening_factor: Coarsening, in [0, 1]. Block centres lie b pixels apart, for b equal to dependency_range * coarsening_factor rounded up to an odd integer. 0 computes the window of every pixel; larger values compute fewer windows, and run faster.

    Example

    The subclasses share these arguments:

    from jaxscape import (
    ConnectivityAnalysis,
    ResistanceDistance,
    SensitivityAnalysis,
)

settings = dict(
    quality_raster=quality,
    permeability_raster=permeability,
    distance=ResistanceDistance(),  # GeometricMultigridCGSolver
    dependency_range=20,
    batch_size=100,
    coarsening_factor=0.25,  # blocks of 5 x 5 pixels
)
connectivity = ConnectivityAnalysis(**settings).run()
d_permeability = SensitivityAnalysis(**settings).run("permeability")

    Connectivity¤

    jaxscape.connectivity_analysis.ConnectivityAnalysis ¤

    Connectivity of a landscape raster: the sum of the proximities of its pairs of pixels.

    With quality weights, the connectivity is

    \[C = \sum_i \sum_{j \neq i} q_i \, K_{ij} \, q_j\]

    where \(q_i\) is habitat quality at pixel \(i\) and \(K_{ij}\) is the proximity between pixels \(i\) and \(j\), a function proximity of their distance \(d_{ij}\) computed in the window centred on pixel \(i\), or in a tile that holds it (see WindowedAnalysis). Pairs farther apart than dependency_range \(D\) along either axis do not contribute.

    By default, \(K_{ij} = e^{-r_{ij}/\alpha}\) with \(\alpha = D/4\), where \(r_{ij}\) is the distance in equivalent pixels: the number of pixels through habitat of permeability 1 that gives the distance \(d_{ij}\). This is the negative-exponential dispersal kernel of metapopulation theory (Hanski & Ovaskainen, 2000). In two dimensions, its mean dispersal distance is \(2\alpha = D/2\), and 91 % of its mass lies within distance \(4\alpha = D\) of the source.

    With coarsening, each pixel takes the term \(\sum_{j \neq c} K_{cj} q_j\) of block centres \(c\), interpolated with the weights \(T_c(i)\) of WindowedAnalysis:

    \[C \approx \sum_c \Big(\sum_i T_c(i) \, q_i\Big) \sum_{j \neq c} K_{cj} \, q_j\]

    The pairs of pixels within a block contribute to this sum, so that it needs no calibration.

    The analysis computes distances window by window, so that its memory does not grow with the size of the raster. See WindowedAnalysis for the windows, coarsening, padding at the raster edges and the permeability floor.

    Distance units

    A custom proximity receives distances in the units of distance:

    • EuclideanDistance: pixels.
    • LCPDistance: the sum of the edge costs 1 / a along the path, for the conductance a of each edge, the mean permeability of its two pixels; hence pixels at permeability 1.
    • ResistanceDistance: effective resistance, with edge resistances 1 / a. It grows like the logarithm of the pixel distance: at permeability 1 in a window of 41 pixels a side, it is 0.50, 1.28 and 1.89 at 1, 10 and 20 pixels from the centre. A kernel exp(-d / c) of the resistance therefore decays as a power law in pixels, and the window edge sets its range.
    • FreeEnergyDistance: from the least-cost path distance (large theta) to a multiple of the resistance (small theta).

    When the analysis is created, the default proximity reads equivalent pixels off one window of permeability 1: the distances from its centre along an axis, interpolated linearly between pixels and in proportion past the window edge. Randomized distances read them off the exact distances that they estimate (see AbstractDistance.exact). For EuclideanDistance and LCPDistance, \(r_{ij} = d_{ij}\). Scale permeability so that 1 is optimal habitat.

    Example

    from jaxscape import ConnectivityAnalysis, LCPDistance

conn = ConnectivityAnalysis(
    quality_raster=quality,
    permeability_raster=permeability,
    distance=LCPDistance(),
    dependency_range=20,  # Window half-width in pixels
    batch_size=1000,
)
connectivity_index = conn.run(q_weighted=True)

    Contiguity

    The graph connects each pixel to its four neighbours along the rows and columns (rook contiguity), and the weight of each edge is the mean permeability of its two pixels.

    run(q_weighted: bool = True) -> Array ¤

    Compute the connectivity metric across the landscape.

    Arguments:

    • q_weighted: If True, weight each pair of pixels by their habitat qualities. If False, sum the proximities alone, with \(q_i = 1\) inside the raster.

    Returns:

    Scalar landscape connectivity value.

    Sensitivity¤

    jaxscape.connectivity_analysis.SensitivityAnalysis ¤

    Sensitivity of connectivity to the quality or permeability of each pixel.

    Returns \(\partial C / \partial q_i\) or \(\partial C / \partial p_i\): how the connectivity \(C\) of ConnectivityAnalysis responds to the habitat quality or permeability of each pixel. Parameters, distance units and the default proximity are those of ConnectivityAnalysis.

    Without coarsening, the result is the gradient of ConnectivityAnalysis.run with the same arguments, computed by automatic differentiation. With SpielmanApproximation, the gradient with respect to permeability is computed from an estimate of the gradient of the exact resistances (see ResistanceDistance.sources_to_windows_distance).

    With coarsening, the windows of block centres \(c\), with interpolation weights \(T_c(i)\) and blocks of \(b\) pixels a side (see WindowedAnalysis), estimate the gradient.

    Quality. With q_weighted and a symmetric distance (see AbstractDistance.symmetric), the derivative is \(2 \sum_{j \neq i} K_{ij} q_j\), interpolated from the block centres without differentiation:

    \[\frac{\partial C}{\partial q_i} \approx 2 \sum_c T_c(i) \sum_{j \neq c} K_{cj} q_j\]

    Otherwise, the result is the gradient of the coarsened connectivity.

    Permeability. With a distance that averages sources (see AbstractDistance.averages_sources), such as ResistanceDistance, the result is the gradient of

    \[\sum_c \Big(\sum_i T_c(i)\, q_i\Big) \sum_j \big(1 - u_c(j)\big)\, K\Big(\frac{\bar d_{cj}}{1 - u_c(j)}\Big)\, q_j, \qquad \bar d_{cj} = \sum_i u_c(i)\, d_{ij}, \qquad u_c(i) = \frac{T_c(i)\, q_i}{\sum_k T_c(k)\, q_k},\]

    over the pixels \(j\) of the window of \(c\) with \(u_c(j) < 1\), where \(u_c\) is the block source of \(c\): the pixels that \(c\) stands for, weighted as in its sum. \(\bar d_{cj}\), the mean of their distances to \(j\), comes from one window query, and \(\bar d_{cj} / (1 - u_c(j))\) is the mean without \(j\) itself, as \(d_{jj} = 0\). For a proximity affine in the distance, the sum over \(j\) is \(\sum_i u_c(i) \sum_{j \neq i} K(d_{ij})\, q_j\): each pixel counts its pairs in the windows of its block centres.

    With other distances, the gradient of \(\sum_c \big(\sum_i T_c(i)\, q_i\big) \sum_{j \notin \text{block}(c)} K_{cj} q_j\), without the pairs within blocks, is multiplied pixel by pixel by a block correction adapted from that of Omniscape:

    \[\kappa(x) = \frac{\sum_c \sum_s w_c(s)\, g(x - c - s)} {\sum_c g_\text{far}(x - c) \sum_s w_c(s)}, \qquad w_c(s) = T_c(c + s).\]

    The weights \(w_c(s)\) select the pixel windows that block centre \(c\) stands for. \(g\) and \(g_\text{far}\) are the permeability gradients of \(\sum_{j \neq c} K_{cj}\) and \(\sum_{j \notin \text{block}(c)} K_{cj}\) for one source at the centre of a window of quality 1 and of the raster's maximum permeability, computed once per run. \(g_\text{far}\) is set to 0 where its magnitude is at most 1e-6 times its largest, and \(\kappa = 1\) where the denominator is 0, as where proximities underflow.

    Limitations

    • Coarsened sensitivities are approximate. The quality term falls steeply within dependency_range of the raster edges, where the interpolation underestimates it.
    • With block sources and a proximity convex in the distance, such as the default, the proximity of the mean distance is at most the mean of the proximities: the gap grows for pixels \(j\) near the block, and with the block size relative to the range of the proximity.
    • With distances that do not average sources, in a uniform landscape away from its edges, the corrected permeability gradient of a block window is the weighted sum of the gradients of its pixel windows, except in the band of up to \(b - 1\) pixels that they reach beyond it, whose gradients are lost. The block correction is biased near the raster edges, on tiles larger than windows, whose graphs extend beyond the windows, and around barriers, where it leaves marks along the lattice of block centres.
    • Randomized distances give noisy sensitivities to permeability where permeability varies over orders of magnitude: lower epsilon, or compare seeds.

    Example

    import jax.numpy as jnp
from jaxscape import ResistanceDistance, SensitivityAnalysis

sens = SensitivityAnalysis(
    quality_raster=quality,
    permeability_raster=permeability,
    distance=ResistanceDistance(),
    dependency_range=20,
    batch_size=100,
)

d_quality = sens.run("quality", q_weighted=True)
d_permeability = sens.run("permeability", q_weighted=True)

# Identify conservation priorities
high_impact = d_quality > jnp.percentile(d_quality, 90)

    run(var: str = 'quality', q_weighted: bool = True) -> Array ¤

    Compute the gradient of connectivity with respect to a raster.

    Arguments:

    • var: Parameter to differentiate: "quality" or "permeability".
    • q_weighted: If True, differentiate the connectivity weighted by habitat quality. If False, differentiate the sum of proximities alone.

    Returns:

    Gradient raster with the shape of the input rasters: the sensitivity of connectivity at each pixel. Pixels whose permeability is at or below the permeability floor have a sensitivity to permeability of zero.

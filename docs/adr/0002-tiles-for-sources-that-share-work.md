# Tiles for sources that share work

Windowed analyses compute a block centre's distances on the graph of its window, or of a tile: the union of the windows of a grid of block centres, whose distances one window query computes. A distance declares with the class attribute `sources_share_work` whether a query from several sources costs much less than one query per source; the analyses read it and never check distance types. Resistance sets it: a query prepares one solver state for the tile's Laplacian, a factorization with a direct solver or the stencil from which `GridMultigridCGSolver` builds a multigrid hierarchy, and solves for every pixel of the tile whatever the number of sources, and a Spielman query embeds every pixel with the same solves. LCP and Euclidean distances keep one window per block centre, batched with `vmap`: on a shared tile, LCP would do about 8 times more work per source. `batch_size` is the largest number of block centres processed together in both cases, and `batch_size=1` gives every distance its own window.

Tiles hold up to `batch_size` block centres, and with at least as many as the raster has, one tile covers the padded raster. A tile graph extends beyond each window, so its distances are lower, and they differ with the place of a block centre in the tile. Spec #36 first kept the block centres of a tile within a tenth of the dependency range, which held the permeability sensitivity of exact resistance within a Pearson correlation of 0.9991 of windows on the Maryland land cover with `dependency_range` 30. With `dependency_range` 100 and blocks of 21 pixels, that limit gave every block centre its own window, and per-window domains cost about 47 times more: with `SpielmanApproximation(epsilon=0.1)` and `CuDSSSolver` in float32 on an RTX 3090, the connectivity took 137 s with a window per block centre, against 2.9 s on one tile, which raised the total connectivity by 3.1 %. Large tiles are the decision.

A raster that needs several tiles has seams along their edges, where neighbouring block centres see different graphs. On Maryland with `dependency_range` 100, blocks of 21 pixels, `SpielmanApproximation(epsilon=0.1)` and `CuDSSSolver` in float32, `batch_size=400` gives 2 x 2 tiles of 18 x 18 block centres, against one tile at `batch_size=4096`. Across the tile edges along columns, the mean absolute step of the logarithm of the permeability sensitivity was 1.79 times that between other neighbouring pixels, against 0.94 at the same positions on one tile; the maps of four tiles and of one tile had a Pearson correlation of 0.9946, and four tiles raised the total sensitivity by 0.74 %. `batch_size` trades memory for seams: a tile holds the distances of up to `batch_size` sources, and fewer, larger tiles leave fewer seams.

Analyses query the distances of block centres through `AbstractDistance.sources_to_windows_distance`, the distances from each source to the pixels of its window; the default proximity reads its profile off one sources-to-targets query on a window of uniform permeability. The default window query slices the distances from the sources to every vertex, which Euclidean, free-energy and exact resistance distances keep: on a tile of `n` vertices, it holds the `m * n` distances of `m` sources besides the memory of the solver, 99 million distances for each of the 2 x 2 tiles of Maryland at `batch_size=1024`, and 1.04 billion, 3.9 GiB in float32, for one tile. LCP overrides it on grid graphs for speed: Bellman–Ford relaxes the distance rasters of all sources by shifting them, without the gathers and scatters of sparse edges, and the LCP connectivity of Maryland took 0.6 s against 4.3 s on an RTX 3090. A Spielman query on a tile of the whole raster would hold the embeddings of every pixel, 4.2 GiB on Maryland, or the distances from every source to every pixel: it overrides the window query to solve its projections a batch at a time and to add the squared differences of their potentials over each window, so that it holds one batch of potentials and the windows. The engine stays agnostic of distances.

The gradient of Spielman estimates whose projections all block centres of a tile share varies much more than the estimates: on one tile of Maryland with `dependency_range` 100, two seeds gave permeability sensitivities with a Pearson correlation of 0.31 at `epsilon` 0.1. The window query therefore pulls the cotangent of its estimates back through exact resistances, which independent random probes of the pairs of a source and a pixel of its window estimate without bias. On the full Maryland raster with `dependency_range` 100, blocks of 21 pixels, one tile and `CuDSSSolver` in float32, two seeds then gave permeability sensitivities with Pearson correlations of 0.9983, 0.9962 and 0.9862 at `epsilon` 0.1, 0.15 and 0.2, and normalized maps, the sensitivity divided by that of a flat landscape, 0.9979, 0.9930 and 0.9853. On a crop of Maryland with `dependency_range` 30 and `epsilon` 0.2, two seeds agreed to 0.96, against 0.16 with the gradient of the estimates, in half the time, and to 0.99 at `epsilon` 0.1.

Spec #36 measured the alternatives. Batching windows into a block-diagonal system gains nothing for sparse solvers, which a single window already saturates, and multiplies memory. A margin of graph around tiles lowered the correlation of exact resistance with windows to 0.975 to 0.947 at `dependency_range` 30, and tiles overlapping by half and blended with tapered weights removed seams at 3.4 times the cost. Randomized distances correct the proximity of their estimates through `AbstractDistance.debiased_proximity`, and the default proximity is read off the distance that they estimate, `AbstractDistance.exact`, so that the engine stays agnostic there too: one randomized profile biased Spielman connectivity by +16 % at `epsilon` 0.1 and +37 % at 0.2. The block correction is calibrated on a window graph: on a tile graph, the calibration of a tile of the whole raster is a gradient of the whole raster, which ran out of memory.

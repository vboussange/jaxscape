# Tiles for sources that share work

Windowed analyses compute a block centre's distances on the graph of its window, or of a tile: the union of the windows of a grid of block centres, whose distances one window query computes. A distance declares with the class attribute `sources_share_work` whether a query from several sources costs much less than one query per source; the analyses read it and never check distance types. Resistance sets it: a query factorizes the tile's Laplacian once and solves for every pixel of the tile whatever the number of sources, and a Spielman query embeds every pixel with the same solves. LCP and Euclidean distances keep one window per block centre, batched with `vmap`: on a shared tile, LCP would do about 8 times more work per source. `batch_size` is the largest number of block centres processed together in both cases, and `batch_size=1` gives every distance its own window.

Tiles hold up to `batch_size` block centres, and with at least as many as the raster has, one tile covers the padded raster. A tile graph extends beyond each window, so its distances are lower, and they differ with the place of a block centre in the tile. Spec #36 first kept the block centres of a tile within a tenth of the dependency range, which held the permeability sensitivity of exact resistance within a Pearson correlation of 0.9991 of windows on the Maryland land cover with `dependency_range` 30. With `dependency_range` 100 and blocks of 21 pixels, that limit gave every block centre its own window, and per-window domains cost about 100 times more: a Spielman connectivity took 137 s on an RTX 3090 in float32, against 1.1 s on one tile, which raised the total connectivity by 3.1 %. Large tiles are the decision, and `batch_size` sets the trade-off.

Analyses query distances through `AbstractDistance.sources_to_windows_distance`, the distances from each source to the pixels of its window. Its default slices the distances from the sources to every vertex, which LCP, Euclidean, free-energy and exact resistance distances keep. A Spielman query on a tile of the whole raster would hold the embeddings of every pixel, 4.2 GiB on Maryland, or the distances from every source to every pixel: it overrides the window query to solve its projections a batch at a time and to add the squared differences of their potentials over each window, so that it holds one batch of potentials and the windows. The engine stays agnostic of distances.

The gradient of Spielman estimates whose projections all block centres of a tile share varies much more than the estimates: on one tile of Maryland with `dependency_range` 100, two seeds gave permeability sensitivities with a Pearson correlation of 0.31 at `epsilon` 0.1. The window query therefore pulls the cotangent of its estimates back through exact resistances, which independent random probes of the pairs of a source and a pixel of its window estimate without bias. On a crop of Maryland with `dependency_range` 30 and `epsilon` 0.2, two seeds then agreed to 0.96 instead of 0.16, in half the time, and to 0.99 at `epsilon` 0.1.

Spec #36 measured the alternatives. Batching windows into a block-diagonal system gains nothing for sparse solvers, which a single window already saturates, and multiplies memory. A margin of graph around tiles lowered the correlation of exact resistance with windows to 0.975 to 0.947 at `dependency_range` 30, and tiles overlapping by half and blended with tapered weights removed seams at 3.4 times the cost. Randomized distances correct the proximity of their estimates through `AbstractDistance.debiased_proximity`, and the default proximity is read off the distance that they estimate, `AbstractDistance.exact`, so that the engine stays agnostic there too: one randomized profile biased Spielman connectivity by +16 % at `epsilon` 0.1 and +37 % at 0.2. The block correction is calibrated on a window graph: on a tile graph, the calibration of a tile of the whole raster is a gradient of the whole raster, which ran out of memory.
